# Research method, claim register, and limitations

The evidence was reviewed through **10 November 2026**. Subsequent editorial revisions broaden the framing and improve presentation without implying a new round of source verification.

## Research question

How will businesses acquire customers through traditional and AI-assisted search over the next decade, and what should an operator do now without depending on unsupported forecasts?

Subquestions covered distribution, buyer behaviour, lead quality, platform capabilities, local services, ecommerce, publishers, B2B, regional market differences, economics, regulation, and possible futures.

## Method used

This study synthesizes public-source research. Discovery and cross-checking covered original platform documentation, research-provider publications, company disclosures, and government records. Source selection favoured primary documents for technical claims and original studies for behavioural claims.

The work followed three analytical passes:

1. **Establish the present:** identify accessible platforms, current documentation, empirical measurements, and commercial mechanics.
2. **Challenge the interpretation:** look for recovery in CTR, higher-value referrals, product reversals, regional differences, and mismatched denominators.
3. **Build decisions:** translate the evidence into conditional playbooks, transparent economics, scenarios, and experiments that could falsify the recommendations.

No proprietary customer analytics were obtained. No merchants were interviewed. No live advertising campaign, buyer survey, platform integration, or controlled business experiment was performed. The examples and workbook are proposed work, not completed field research.

## Evidence classification

The following distinctions guide the analysis. They are expressed through citations, wording, and section context rather than repeated labels before each paragraph.

| Category | Meaning | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | A cited source reports the observation or rule | Inspect source scope before applying |
| Analysis | The author's interpretation of one or more observations | Challenge assumptions and alternatives |
| Recommendation | A proposed action | Validate in a specific business |
| Scenario | A conditional future | Monitor indicators; do not treat as a forecast |
| Illustration | Invented inputs or fictional example | Replace with observed data before investment |

Confidence refers to the narrow claim, not the authority of the entire document. An official page is high-quality evidence of a platform's stated eligibility but does not independently demonstrate that the platform improves customer profit.

## Contradictions and version changes resolved

| Apparent conflict | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Google grows while publishers report losses | Revenue and publisher referrals measure different outcomes; no arithmetic contradiction |
| Lower CTR versus higher AI revenue per visit | Exposure-to-click behaviour differs from value conditional on a visit |
| Ahrefs decline versus Seer recovery | Different periods, samples, and definitions; neither cancels the other |
| Older statements that Search Console has no separate AI report | Specific 2026 reporting documentation supersedes that operational assumption |
| AI commerce announcements versus limited geography | Product announcement is not global account availability |
| Older ChatGPT Instant Checkout positioning versus newer merchant checkout flexibility | Use March 2026 strategy update and current product documentation |
| Perplexity merchant search snippet versus current destination | Record redirect and mark programme access unresolved |
| India data from an ambiguous Statcounter URL | Reject mis-scoped page; use `/search-engine-market-share/all/india` |
| Company summaries of beneficial user behaviour versus a critical field experiment | Different designs and incentives; inspect experimental limits and avoid a universal verdict |

## Quantitative checks

The report's business examples were checked arithmetically. The illustrative earlier funnel yields `10,000 × .02 × .40 × .20 = 16` customers; the alternative yields `6,000 × .03 × .60 × .20 = 21.6`. Using the book's currency-neutral example, multiplication by CU 5,000 gives CU 80,000 and CU 108,000 before acquisition. A 40% traffic fall requires approximately 1.667 times the conversion rate to preserve the same customer count, all else equal.

The source figures are attributed observations; the original private datasets were not independently reconstructed. Changes measured in percentage points are not interchangeable with relative percentage changes.

## Limits of this edition

- Broad global synthesis, with stronger evidence for English-language and US markets than for many regional markets.
- Selected current pages and studies, not a systematic review with exhaustive database screening.
- Provider analytics may contain selection, attribution, and disclosure biases.
- Dynamic documentation and market tables can change after the access date; no independent archival snapshot of every page was created.
- One research paper is a preprint; acceptance and replication were not established.
- No national lead-cost, conversion, income, or time-to-ranking benchmark is invented.
- No account-level feature eligibility or paid tool pricing is promised.
- Source readability is not the same as having access to all underlying data.

## Excluded or limited material

News and marketing summaries were used to discover some originals but were not substituted for the primary documents listed. Mirrored domains were avoided when originals were available. Unsupported claims of guaranteed GEO ranking, fabricated statistics, and exact distant-year forecasts were excluded. Bing's general guideline page returned a JavaScript shell; the book instead relies on readable official reporting and protocol documentation for its specific claims.

## Update procedure

Reopen the relevant original source, record the access date and changed scope, update the affected claim, and explain the recommendation change in the change log. Retain a historical note when a major product or method changes. Seek new empirical evidence before changing the confidence of behavioural claims merely because a platform announces a feature.
