## 5. Lead and outcome definitions

| Stage | Definition to agree before measurement |
|---|---|
| Enquiry | A genuine request for information about the offer |
| Valid lead | An enquiry after removing spam and duplicates |
| Qualified lead | A valid lead meeting documented fit criteria |
| Opportunity | A qualified lead with a concrete purchase process |
| Won sale | An accepted purchase or agreement under the business's definition |
| Collected sale | Payment received; track refunds separately |
| Net contribution | Collected revenue less the explicitly included variable and acquisition costs |

Use one unique lead ID. Log source, landing destination, need, timestamps, status, loss reason, sale value, cost, and collection. A repeated enquiry from the same purchase process is not automatically a new lead.

### Proposed experiment A: better qualification

Make offer scope, service area, or product compatibility clearer. Compare rejected-enquiry reasons and accepted opportunities over comparable periods. Track total demand as well as qualification rate: filtering out useful buyers would be a failure even if the percentage improves.

### Proposed experiment B: product-information accuracy

Correct a small, commercially meaningful group of products. Keep a comparable unchanged group when practical. Record corrections, eligibility, exposure, orders, returns, and inventory changes. Do not attribute improvement to the feed alone if promotions or stock also changed.

### Proposed experiment C: buyer evidence

Add a genuine demonstration or documented example to a relevant commercial destination. Observe enquiry quality and conversion. Keep the offer and distribution reasonably stable. If traffic is too low to distinguish effects, report that limitation instead of declaring a win.

### Proposed experiment D: second discovery route

Prepare an existing accurate asset for a relevant additional search service or directory. Track incremental maintenance cost and new accepted demand. Avoid counting a customer twice if it was already in the pipeline.

## 10. Decision memo

Write one paragraph describing what was learned, what remains unknown, which action is justified, its maximum cost, and when the decision will be revisited. Attach the evidence and the arithmetic. Do not replace the decision with a list of activity metrics.
